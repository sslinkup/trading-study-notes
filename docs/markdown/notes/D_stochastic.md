# Naked RSI, Stochastic RSI and the NKED Method

*Original reference notes. The linked videos are the original sources and remain their authors' property. Click a timestamp to verify any claim at its source.*

## 1. First principles

The method assumes the market is imperfect and heavily influenced by large participants. That is why it avoids precision: levels are zones, not exact numbers, and a reading is evidence about the state of the market rather than a signal that must be obeyed. Indicators are guides, not instructions.

The overall aim is to describe the *energy* behind a move — how much room is left, whether momentum is confirming price, and whether the higher timeframe supports the lower-timeframe story.

Sources: *How to NAKEDRSI* ([03:13], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bj8tpCSfjdI); *Stochastic RSI — Deeper Breakdown* ([01:57], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOXOgQCX5f4); *NKED Method* ([10:07], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pVHd_vf-KX8).

## 2. Settings and thresholds

### RSI ("naked RSI")

Use the default RSI. The moving-average baseline can be removed or kept; when kept, a cross above it is one signal among others. The health levels used throughout the material are **36, 46, 56 and 65**:

- **46–56** is the neutral range. Inside it, direction is close to a coin flip.
- **Above 56** adds bullish confirmation; moving back below 56 weakens that confirmation.
- **Below 46** starts to turn bearish.
- **36** is a soft bearish level — "somewhat" bearish rather than extreme. A break of 36 on a higher timeframe is treated as meaningful damage to the bull structure.
- **65** acts as a ceiling where pushes are often rejected, particularly when price has run far above it.
- Classic 70/30 extremes are acknowledged but treated as secondary. Historical examples cited include very low readings near 9–25 and highs near 87 or 95–99.

### Stochastic RSI

Classic stochastic RSI is bounded between 0 and 100. The conventional 80/20 bands are noted, but the material explicitly downgrades them relative to RSI: stochastic RSI can stay oversold or overbought for a long time, so the label alone is a weak signal.

The **fast stochastic RSI** is a modified version with settings **3369**. Its stated purpose is to react sooner than the normal stochastic, and the comparison between fast and slow is used to judge whether a move is losing energy. A rejection zone around 40–60 is mentioned for the fast version.

Sources: *Lesson Number 5 — Stochastic RSI and Keltner Channels* ([01:57], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7MmCcxadAwQ); *NKED Method* ([17:08], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pVHd_vf-KX8); ([18:12], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pVHd_vf-KX8).

### EMAs, Ichimoku, Keltner and Watson

- **9 EMA** — short-term regime marker. Price below it suggests a weaker short-term picture.
- **50 EMA** — a level that strong bullish moves tend to respect.
- **100 EMA** — described as the trend gatekeeper across timeframes; holding above it is the main condition for an uptrend.
- **200 EMA** — the final structural line that major moves are expected to respect.
- **Ichimoku Cloud** — used to classify the state of the market: above the cloud is bullish, below is bearish, inside is turbulent.
- **Keltner channels** — default multiplier 1 on lower timeframes, around 2 on higher timeframes; the midline is used for rotation reads.
- **Watson envelope** — an outer band; the rule is that it should sit outside the Keltner. A green line entering the Keltner is treated as bearish, a red line entering it as bullish.

## 3. Reading RSI health

The 46–56 band defines the middle of the market. When price pushes above 56 and holds, the move has confirmation; when RSI slips back under 46, the burden of proof shifts to the bears. A reading at 36 is bearish but not hopeless, and a failure at 65 after a strong run is used as evidence of exhaustion.

The RSI trend line is drawn the same way as a price trend line. A break of that line is treated as a change in the character of the move, often earlier than the equivalent break on price.

Sources: *How to NAKEDRSI* ([06:02], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bj8tpCSfjdI); ([28:09], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bj8tpCSfjdI); *The Basic of TA — Setting Up Charts* ([50:02], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zv8pXI_7VJY).

## 4. Overbought and oversold as states

The single most important correction in the material is that an extreme reading is not a sell or buy signal. An overbought stochastic in a strong uptrend can stay overbought, and an oversold reading in a downtrend can stay oversold. The correct question is what *kind* of overbought or oversold it is — bullish overbought versus exhausted overbought, bearish oversold versus bouncy oversold.

Two practical rules are given. If stochastic RSI falls without ever reaching the oversold zone, that is read as strong bullish behaviour. If oscillators remain extended for roughly eight to ten candles, the probability of a reversal increases.

Sources: *Stochastic RSI — Deeper Breakdown* ([05:35], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOXOgQCX5f4); ([10:52], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOXOgQCX5f4); *Lesson Number 5* ([12:12], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7MmCcxadAwQ); *How to Approach Timeframe* ([08:07], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VoIr0w6l8gc).

## 5. Divergence

Divergence is a comparison between price and oscillator, not a standalone entry:

- Higher price with higher RSI is healthy.
- Higher price with lower RSI warns that the move is losing support.
- Lower price with higher RSI can mark bearish exhaustion.
- Rising successive stochastic bottoms while price holds or rises is bullish; successive lower highs in the oscillator while price rises is bearish.

A trend-change gate is emphasised: a bearish divergence is not acted on until price has returned to the overbought area and failed to make a significantly higher high. The worked example contrasts a stochastic top near 48k with RSI around 56 and a later top near 25k with RSI around 46.

Sources: *Stochastic RSI — Deeper Breakdown* ([02:31], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOXOgQCX5f4); ([03:05], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOXOgQCX5f4); ([14:55], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOXOgQCX5f4); *Lesson Number 5* ([07:42], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7MmCcxadAwQ).

## 6. Multi-timeframe method

The timeframe ladder is built on three-minute, nine-minute and twenty-seven-minute candles, with the "times nine" rule used to translate a candle on one timeframe into roughly nine candles on the one below. Higher timeframes (daily, three-day, weekly, monthly) define the story; lower timeframes provide the entry.

The recommended learning order is to start high and work down. A trader who begins on the three-minute chart learns noise instead of context.

## 7. Keltner channels and the envelope

Keltner bands act as a magnet. Price that moves outside them tends to be pulled back toward the middle; in a strong bear move it may instead travel sideways in an L-shape until the bands catch up. Rotation is described as top → middle → bottom, with a break of the middle opening a move toward the opposite band.

The 9 EMA combined with the bands is used as a regime signal: a 9 EMA below the lower band on the weekly is read as strongly bearish; above the upper band as strongly bullish.

## 8. NKED method in steps

1. Classify the regime with Ichimoku and the higher-timeframe EMAs.
2. Read RSI health against 36 / 46 / 56 / 65.
3. Compare the fast stochastic (3369) with the slow one to gauge energy.
4. Check divergences against price and require a failed return to an extreme before acting.
5. Locate Keltner and Watson extremes for mean-reversion context.
6. Require volume confirmation on the instrument and on Bitcoin/Ethereum.
7. Drop to the lower timeframe only to time the entry inside the higher-timeframe story.
8. Place the stop at the structural invalidation level.
9. Take a partial close into strength and let the remainder ride with a wider stop.
10. Record the decision in the journal and review it against the original story.

Sources: *NKED Method* ([17:08], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pVHd_vf-KX8); ([56:33], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pVHd_vf-KX8); *Lesson Number 5* ([08:52], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7MmCcxadAwQ).

## 9. Failure modes

The named errors are: selling an overbought reading without regime context, treating the oscillator as a guarantee, ignoring the higher timeframe, drawing trend lines on stochastic RSI before gaining experience, extending stops when the story has not changed, and using indicators as a crutch rather than as one input among several.
