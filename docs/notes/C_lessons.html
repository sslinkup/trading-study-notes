Trading Lessons and Philosophy

Original study notes. The linked videos are the original sources and remain their authors' property. Click any timestamp to open the exact moment.

1. The reality of the game

Trading is presented as an activity where making money is easier than keeping it. A correct guess is common; repeated correct decisions with controlled size are not. The metaphor used in the lessons is a set of doors: every setup opens several possible futures, and price closes some of them as it moves. A good trade is one where the chosen branch was more likely and the downside was defined before entry.

Two characters are contrasted. One style takes profits, keeps them, and moves on. The other holds for more, gives the gain back, and then trades emotionally to recover it. The difference is behaviour, not analysis.

Sources: Lesson 1 — Introduction to Trading ([00:04], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LKJPwG14y2M); ([04:30], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LKJPwG14y2M); ([13:34], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LKJPwG14y2M).

2. Where the edge comes from

Concentration is the central theme. Pick one major coin — the material suggests one of the larger names — and learn its behaviour across timeframes. Knowing one instrument's rhythm, volume patterns, and key areas is treated as more valuable than shallow knowledge of twenty.

Other sources of edge listed in the lessons:

  • Recognising recurring patterns rather than predicting a single outcome.
  • Reading market emotion and energy: where participants feel strongly, price tends to react.
  • Understanding how the chosen exchange behaves, including its funding and liquidation mechanics.
  • Treating Bitcoin dominance as context for the rest of the market.

Sources: Lesson 2 — Alpha That Will Save You Thousands ([28:41], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rLdY77E8KzA); The Basic of TA — Setting Up Charts ([29:56], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zv8pXI_7VJY).

3. The happy decision

A decision is judged by whether it can be lived with calmly. "Happy trading" in these notes means not focusing on the money while the trade is open: the entry, invalidation, and target were decided earlier, so the only remaining job is to follow them. A trade that produces anxiety was probably mis-sized or poorly planned.

Sources: Lesson 3 — Trade Strategy ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WAUnwtRBE8); Mastering Yourself ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gJNO11fkK_Y).

4. Rules and discipline

Written rules cover entries, exits, size, daily limits, and when to stop for the day. They are useful precisely because they were written when calm and are followed when the market is not. A checklist converts them into a repeatable pre-trade routine.

The three-strike convention appears across the lessons: if the first few setups fail, stop rather than forcing a fourth. A constant bet size is part of the same discipline — no doubling after a loss and no size increase after a win.

Sources: The Key to Success ([19:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UfxEboq-tOM); Lesson 3 — Trade Strategy ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8WAUnwtRBE8).

5. Stops and invalidation

A stop loss marks where the idea is wrong, not how much money the trader is prepared to lose. Place it at a structural level — a recent wick, a range boundary, an EMA on the relevant timeframe — and accept that a sweep of obvious stops is part of the game. Moving a stop further away to avoid taking a loss converts a defined trade into an open-ended bet.

Sources: Guide to Stop Losses — Introduction ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yNhmxEc6y5c); Key Thoughts Going Into 2026 ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ti3JhXEY9UM).

6. Taking profit and adding back

Partial closes are planned in advance. A common pattern is to bank a substantial part of the move, then let the remainder run with a wider stop. Adding back is only discussed in the context of a trade already in profit and with a new invalidation level; adding to a losing position is not part of the method.

The warning against turning a winner into a loser is repeated often. A small, certain gain is preferred to a larger possible gain that requires perfect timing.

Sources: Lesson 5 — Leverage, Taking Profit, Thinking Ahead ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UscLoywpZR0); Lesson 4 — Starting Trading from Scratch ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pZOQbD_7UAc).

7. Leverage

Leverage changes the room a position has, not the quality of the idea. The material compares high leverage on a small account with low leverage on a large one: the notional exposure can be identical. What matters is whether the stop fits inside the price noise and whether the trader can follow the plan without panic.

Sources: Lesson 5 — Leverage, Taking Profit, Thinking Ahead ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UscLoywpZR0); Basic Explanation of EMAs Part 1 ([30:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5hiv8vzMxcA).

8. Shorting

Shorting is treated as a separate skill with its own mindset. The examples emphasise waiting for confirmation on the higher timeframe, placing the stop above a structural level, and taking partial profit into downward moves. The same rule applies: a short opened without an invalidation level is a bet, not a trade.

Sources: The Art of Shorting — Part 1 ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-RFNrX5GNoc).

9. The chart toolkit

The tools used together are:

  • Horizontal levels drawn at high-volume areas, extended forward rather than erased.
  • RSI read with health levels rather than fixed 70/30 rules.
  • Stochastic RSI for energy and divergence, including the fast variant.
  • A stack of EMAs (9, 50, 100, 200) for regime and gatekeeping.
  • Ichimoku for the state of the market.
  • Keltner channels and the Watson envelope for extremes and mean reversion.

Timeframes form a ladder. A lower-timeframe entry is only meaningful when it agrees with the story on the higher timeframe.

Sources: The Basic of TA — Setting Up Charts ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zv8pXI_7VJY); Lesson Number 5 — Stochastic RSI and Keltner Channels ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7MmCcxadAwQ); How to Approach Timeframe ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VoIr0w6l8gc).

10. Bankroll and warnings

Start small, keep size constant, separate long-term holdings from trading capital, and withdraw profits. Every lesson returns to the same point: the edge is mostly behavioural. The trader who follows a modest plan consistently will do better than the trader with a brilliant setup and no rules.

Sources: Becoming a Trader Part 2 — Bankroll Management ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0LFo5f1TtHA); Introduction — New to This YouTube? ([08:32], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JKLiG3p-qN4).
