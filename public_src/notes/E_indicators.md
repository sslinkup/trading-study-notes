# Indicators and Chart Reading

## 1. What technical analysis is for

Technical analysis is treated as a way of assigning probabilities, not as a way of knowing the future. The market can be read as close to a coin flip on any single trade; the edge comes from context, concentration, and consistent decision-making. Confidence and self-belief are prerequisites, because a trader who does not trust their own process will not follow it.

## 2. Chart setup: levels and volume clusters

The first tool is the horizontal line. Draw lines at obvious highs and lows and extend them indefinitely into the future — a level does not stop mattering because it was drawn last month. Areas where a lot of volume changed hands are called "areas of battle": a zone where buyers and sellers repeatedly fought, and therefore a zone price is likely to revisit.

Volume bars at the bottom of the chart are part of the same read. A candle body moving through a low-volume area is weaker evidence than one moving through a high-volume area. Wick and body are read separately: a long wick into a level followed by a close back inside shows rejection.

Worked examples in the sources include the 3,900–3,970 area in ETH, the 4,000 level, and a first weekly close above 4,000 that opened a move toward 4,800. In BTC, a 1,928 line of best fit on the four-hour chart marked the break that the daily chart hid.

## 3. Trend lines and timeframes

Trend lines are drawn on price and on oscillators in the same way: connect the relevant sequence of highs or lows and watch for a break of the line. Multiple possible paths should be sketched rather than one forecast, so that the trader can react to whichever path price actually takes.

Lower timeframes show detail that daily candles hide. The four-hour chart may reveal a break of a line that looks intact on the daily. Conversely, the higher timeframe decides whether a lower-timeframe signal is worth acting on.

## 4. RSI and stochastic RSI

Textbook RSI rules (above 50 bullish, above 70 overbought, below 30 oversold) are acknowledged but replaced in practice by the health levels 36 / 46 / 56 / 65 described in the dedicated indicator note. Selling a reading simply because it is oversold is specifically discouraged, because oversold can stay oversold.

Stochastic RSI oscillates between 0 and 100 with conventional 80/20 bands. The more useful read is divergence: a stochastic at 100 while RSI has only reached the high 30s or low 30s indicates a weak move. Trend lines drawn on RSI are treated like price trend lines, and a break of the RSI line can precede the price break.

## 5. The EMA stack

- **9 EMA** — the fastest regime marker. Used as the short-term trend setter and in combinations with Keltner bands.
- **30 and 50 EMA** — described as close companions and gatekeepers in bullish moves; the 50 often provides the bounce in a strong trend.
- **100 EMA** — the main trend gatekeeper. As long as price holds above it, the broader uptrend is considered intact.
- **200 EMA** — the final structural line; failure here changes the character of the move.

EMA crossings are covered in a separate lesson. The emphasis in these notes is on price relative to a level as a regime filter, rather than on any single crossover as a mechanical signal.

## 6. Ichimoku and the Keltner/Watson layer

Ichimoku provides the market state: price above the cloud is a bullish environment, below it a bearish one, and inside it a volatile environment where direction is unresolved. The cloud is also described in energy terms — a zone whose boundaries matter for where momentum gathers.

Keltner channels supply mean-reversion context. Price outside the bands is usually pulled back toward the middle, though a strong trend may move sideways until the bands catch up. The Watson envelope sits outside the Keltner; a green line entering the Keltner is bearish, a red line entering it bullish.

Sources: *Ichimoku Cloud — How to Use* ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L4tJI7bWrGc); *Watson Envelope + Keltner* ([00:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5hiv8vzMxcA); *Lesson Number 5* ([29:00], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7MmCcxadAwQ).

## 7. Combining the indicators

The indicators are not independent signals. A single story is assembled: regime from Ichimoku and EMAs, health from RSI, energy from fast and slow stochastic, extremes from Keltner and Watson, and confirmation from volume. A lower-timeframe entry is only taken when it agrees with that story.

Sources: *How to Approach Timeframe* ([07:32], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VoIr0w6l8gc); *NKED Method* ([17:08], https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pVHd_vf-KX8).

## 8. Warnings

Keep a separate long-term portfolio and a small trading portfolio. Do not use high leverage without understanding the sizing implications. Training takes months, and a trader exposed to only one market regime will be unprepared when conditions change. Bitcoin dominance is background context: when Bitcoin turns down, most altcoins follow.
